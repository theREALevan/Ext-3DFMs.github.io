Abstract

3D foundation models (3DFMs) have recently transformed 3D vision, enabling joint prediction of depths, poses, and point maps directly from images. Yet their ability to reason under extreme, non-overlapping views remains largely unexplored.

In this work, we study their internal representations and find that 3DFMs exhibit an emergent understanding of extreme-view geometry, despite never being trained for such conditions. To further enhance these capabilities, we introduce a lightweight alignment scheme that refines their internal 3D representation by tuning only a small subset of backbone bias terms, leaving all decoder heads frozen. This targeted adaptation substantially improves relative pose estimation under extreme viewpoints without degrading per-image depth or point quality.

Additionally, we contribute MegaUnScene, a new benchmark of Internet scenes unseen by existing 3DFMs, with dedicated test splits for both relative pose estimation and dense 3D reconstruction. All code and data will be released.

Method

We analyze how 3DFMs internally represent 3D structure through cross-view attention visualizations, revealing that the shared alternating attention backbone encodes a surprisingly rich understanding of scene geometry. The resulting maps show near-correspondence patterns and symmetry-based cues that persist even when the input images have little or no visual overlap, highlighting that the model's internal 3D language extends well beyond direct pixel-level matches.

Building on this insight, we propose a lightweight alignment framework that fine-tunes 3DFMs for enhanced extreme-view reasoning while preserving their pretrained knowledge. We introduce a rotation-based alignment objective that supervises only on relative camera poses between image pairs using a geodesic loss, avoiding the need for dense point-wise annotations. To prevent disruption of geometric alignment between task-specific heads, we apply minimal fine-tuning by updating only the bias terms within the sparse set of identified backbone layers, leaving all decoder heads frozen. This targeted approach achieves effective alignment of the model's internal 3D language for extreme-view reasoning without degrading per-image depth or point quality, with a 99.99% reduction in trainable parameters compared to full fine-tuning.
